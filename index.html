SkillMap Cohort 1 Tracker

79 candidates · 10-week HVAC certification · weekly risk triage

Weekly risk review on this cohort began as a manual pass across all 79 enrolled candidates. I replaced it with a 47-column tracker in Google Sheets, driven by Apps Script, that scores every candidate against rule-based criteria and classifies them On Track, At Risk, or Not On Track. The weekly pass now starts from a shortlist of 33 flagged cases rather than the full roster, and the same rules generate the end-of-week escalation report that goes to management.

Flagging a case is one decision. Acting on the flag is a different one. When the Chief of Staff proposed full deadline automation, I recommended a manual-review dashboard with a bulk-fail action instead. Automating the flag is cheap. Automating the consequence is not, because a wrong auto-fail costs a candidate their place. The final data settled it: all 45 candidates who passed submitted late and none on time, so automated deadline enforcement would have failed the entire graduating group.

ABB Hurricane packaging optimisation

400 SKUs · 30+ box types · Individual project · Industry brief from ABB

ABB packs its Hurricane switches, 400 models from 1.5 to 95 kg, by hand into more than 30 corrugated box types. After a visit to ABB, I took the dimensions and weights for every switch and box pair and calculated the void percentage for each. Several box families ran at 50 to 74% empty space.

I proposed two concepts. The first is a height-adjustable box with pre-scored crease lines, which also lets two near-identical box families share one footprint. The second is a kirigami honeycomb insert cut from flat corrugated sheet, replacing loose filler. Combined, I estimated about 12% less packaging weight (10% less corrugated board per box, 30% fewer inserts) and roughly 0.3 kg CO2e saved per unit, in a mono-material design aligned with the upcoming PPWR.

Factory cell production simulation

VisualComponents · Human-robot collaboration · University of Vaasa

I built a complete 3D factory cell in Visual Components — warehouse shelf, twin conveyors, vertical mill, coordinate measuring machine, articulated robot and manual assembly — then ran it for an hour and measured output at the sink. The first run gave 55 units per hour, and the constraint was not a machine: one operator was busy almost continuously while parts queued on the conveyors and the robot sat idle inside the fence. A second operator took it to 89, a second manual workstation to 100, and a third operator with reordered transport priorities to 130. The mill, the CMM and the robot were never touched.

Case 04Decision Analysis

Grade 5 / 5

Scenario planning under uncertainty

4 policy alternatives · expected value and Hurwicz · 300 × 300 grid

A simulated Koala Ebola outbreak in a tourist hub with heavy connectivity to Finland, and four policy alternatives spanning the intervention spectrum: minimal travel restrictions, mandatory testing and quarantine, border closure, and internal lockdown. Writing as the Ministry of Health and Wellbeing, I scored all four across four scenarios on a single measure, change in Finnish GDP, with the matrix values justified against COVID-19 non-pharmaceutical intervention studies and tourism's 5.3% share of Finnish GDP in 2019.

Two decision rules, because they answer different questions. The expected value rule carries belief about containment; the Hurwicz rule carries the decision-maker's optimism. Combined with equal weights after affine scaling, then swept across a 300 × 300 grid of containment probability against optimism. Testing and quarantine proved the most robust policy, optimal across the widest combined range. Minimal restrictions win only when containment is likely and the decision-maker is optimistic; internal lockdown only when containment is unlikely and they are pessimistic.
